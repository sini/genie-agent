---
name: before-asking-the-owner
description: Before putting any fork, question or "approve?" to the owner — when to ask, and how to dissolve what is not really their question; also when the owner states a constraint that may already be law
type: practice
---

**Rule:** Ask early, but bring only what only the owner can settle. A question survives only if
standing law and owner testimony do not already decide it, theory and prior art do not, measuring
first would not make it moot, and its premise traces to the owner's VERBATIM words. An arm excluded
by an existing ruling is not an arm. Every fork names what CHANGES under each answer.

**Why:**

- **Timing.** Scope drifts in the gaps between owner contacts; sessions that execute the unambiguous
  while queuing questions compound in the wrong direction. Owner: "putting off input from me allows
  defects to grow and scope to drift." A mechanical tier is a ceremony tier, not a priority tier.
- **Readiness.** A fork that looked measured was inverted by two further passes; ruling early would
  have ruled wrong. Items queued for weeks as "decisions" were really work.
- **Entailment.** Four times in a few days the owner was asked to re-rule something ruled ("Didn't I
  already rule this one?"). A fork sheet's recommendation quoted one of three deciding rules while
  arguing against it ("can easily be deduced against standing law"). A prior-art sweep finds a TOPIC;
  it does not ask whether a ruling ENTAILS the answer. One fork quoted half a ruling and dropped the
  half that closed it.
- **Manufactured forks.** A question about a "reading" that existed only in the orchestrator's own
  wording, added while banking an earlier "agree". Owner: "where's the ambiguity?" Of six forks in one
  sitting a gate judged three defective (ratify a defended derivation; rule on a proposition nobody
  advanced; a false dichotomy). Of five escalations in another session, four were resolvable against a
  stated bar and the fifth dissolved when measured. The day the dissolve-first directive was given,
  six candidates were checked and none survived.
- **Missed linkage.** The owner restated a constraint already in the memory index and tracker; it was
  recorded as a new "clarification". Owner: "why aren't you already aware and reinforcing the linkage?"

**How to apply:**

- Sort every owed item: (1) NOT A RULING (a keystroke, a permission): derive, then ask; (2) GENUINELY
  THE OWNER'S (strategy, direction, a fork law and theory leave open): these outrank the rest;
  (3) RESEARCH DEBT: take it to a recommendation or dissolve it.
- Sweep law files, the memory index and the artefact's own rows first; read the artefact for its own
  answer (twice the dissolving fact was already in it). Name the nearest ruling and what it entails
  about EACH arm. One arm left: execute and report the deduction with grounds. Foreclosure: an arm is
  refuted when taking it makes an existing ruling impossible to perform.
- Single-answer test: if law or theory gives one correct construction, put it as a confirmation,
  quoting the clause.
- Arms are a claim: say how the set was generated and hunt the unnamed arm (often a construction where
  the listed arms are recognition or enumeration). An unnamed arm is the tell that "not a fork" is
  premature; miscalls run that way, executing a design decision the owner did not make.
- When the owner speaks, sweep first. If it is standing law, record a LINK and name the anchors back.
- A dated record and a spec section have no standing priority over each other: find the LATEST ACT on
  the question in both and cite it. Deeper-grilled prior records outrank a same-day recommendation.
- Design text conflicting with law is fixed to conform without asking; raise it only if honouring it
  needs a law AMENDMENT, said plainly. Law changes for a genuine error or advantage, not convenience.
- A fork promoted by a subagent is a relayed claim; test it. Two instances in opposite directions is
  the minimum before writing a direction rule. Report a bounded result as bounded.
- An unanswered offer is not consent; a one-word "agree" to a multi-arm question is not consent to a
  costly arm. How to put what survives: \[[presenting-a-decision]\].
