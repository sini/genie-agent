name: owner-testimony-is-primary
description: When an artefact claims the owner approved, reviewed or decided something; and whenever a claim matters enough to settle.
type: practice
---

**Rule:** The owner's own testimony is primary. An artefact describing what the owner approved is not.
More generally, settle any claim that matters at its primary source, never at a summary, map,
roadmap, index or prior report.

**Why:**

- An orchestrator overturned an architecture record's "concept-approved but never design-approved"
  using a `Status:` line in a roadmap. Owner: "your refutation is against the source of truth: my
  testimony. If I say I didn't review it then I didn't review it." That status line was the agent's
  testimony about the owner, written by the party seeking the approval. A record of consent authored by
  the party who wanted it is not evidence of consent.
- Secondary layers drift in BOTH directions. In one session an archived paper summary carried a
  recommendation the paper does not support; in the same session a different summary was correct
  while a hand-maintained ecosystem map claimed consumers that measure zero and cited a theorem that
  does not exist. The invariant is distance from the primary, not "summaries are unreliable".

**How to apply:**

- An approval, sign-off or ratification claim inside an agent-authored artefact is unverified
  provenance. It cannot overturn the owner's account and cannot be cited as the reason a design is
  settled. Treat every agent-written "approved" as "an agent wrote that it was approved".
- The asymmetry is deliberate: the owner saying "I did not review this" settles it; an artefact saying
  "the owner reviewed this" does not.
- Deference runs two ways and must not blur: toward the assistant on the literature, away from it on
  what the owner decided.
- When two derived layers disagree, do not pick the newer one. Go to the primary: the paper, the
  source file, the owner's words.
