description: When putting a question to the owner in a decision session ("sitting") — menu versus interactive grill, the format of each item, completeness, and channel discipline
type: practice
---

**Rule:** Every question carries background, the question, EVERY arm with its price, and a
recommendation plus counter-case naming arms by letter. Use a menu (one multiple-choice question)
only when the owner already holds the context; anything needing context, or spanning several specs,
is a GRILL: live prose, one question per turn, the fork sheet as ammunition, not the interface.
Answer the owner's questions in the FINAL message of your turn.

**Why:**

- On a five-item batch: three items "don't carry enough information to rule; present one at a time."
  Another: "you didn't present the options, but I could infer from the R vs C what the space was.
  Present the complete set." A recommendation and counter-case show at most two arms.
- A batch of four menu questions was bounced ("these relate to multiple different specs and require
  some context"); more than fourteen rulings then landed in prose. Setting a fork sheet up as menu
  screens drew "aren't grills typically interactive?" The decisive turns of one grill (a lean that
  dissolved under clarification, a reframe toward API alignment) could not have happened via menus.
- Five "defaults already taken" sent as one-liners: shown in full, the owner overrode or reshaped two.
  "Defaulted builds should not cut review corners."
- "Is this actually useful, or is it ceremony?" dissolved an item. After a representation ruling the
  owner asked how often the value was accessed and whether recomputation beat caching: second rounds
  an arm list could have pre-empted. One owner probe ("are we confident?") caught an overconfident
  recommendation before it was ruled.
- An explanation written between tool calls never reached the owner: "It could be in your reasoning,
  but you didn't tell me it."
- About five agents reporting during a session: "the number of active subagents makes our channel
  quite noisy; hold any new rows until the current streams settle."

**How to apply:**

- Format: (1) BACKGROUND, the defect or mechanism in plain terms, every term defined at first use,
  measured figures tagged; (2) the QUESTION, with the standing-law link inside it; (3) every arm,
  lettered, one per line, with its price, foreclosed arms marked with why; (4) R and C by letter;
  (5) one line on how the arms were generated.
- Check each fork for honesty first: it names what changes under each answer, and any refutation in
  it has been re-checked. Add a critical read: is this ceremony, does it unblock work? For
  field/function/caching changes, state demand frequency and recompute-versus-cache cost.
- Before calling something a fork, check whether a ruling in the same session settles it. Owner
  questions mid-item usually reframe the arms: re-derive the arms under that framing.
- Quote measurements as challenges; push back where an answer collides with one. Answer probes
  honestly ("where does (c) win?").
- A provisional answer becomes input, not law, the moment the owner flags formal process. A
  conditional reply is not a ruling (\[[recording-owner-rulings]\]).
- What a pending reading blocks: list only dependents by edge; everything else proceeds. "Saving a
  release run" is not a reason to hold independent landings.
- Owner present: 2-3 agents at most, one-line relays, teammate reports verified and banked silently.
  Once the agenda is done, new forks go to the next session's agenda.
- Never schedule the session yourself. Asked how big it is, give item counts by kind, never a duration.
- Never end on a question that blocks you when a reversible default exists; take it and say so.
