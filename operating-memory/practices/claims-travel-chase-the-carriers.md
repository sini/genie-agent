---
name: claims-travel-chase-the-carriers
description: When renaming, relocating or copying a documented concept, or when striking/correcting a claim that other documents or records cite.
type: practice
---

**Rule:** A rename is a transplant: every claim about the old referent moves onto the new one, where
some are false. A correction is not done until every other carrier of the old claim is struck too.

**Why:**

- In one session, three renames carried false claims: a theory attribution moved onto a driver that
  does nothing the theory describes, landing in a bibliography row; a false "eager topological push"
  attribution travelled into a renamed file; a module move carried two theory claims that failed at
  the primary source. Copied docs inherit their source's citation defects wholesale.
- A spec's false universal was struck through a full review cycle and stayed live in two tracker
  records, including the owner's objective row, which carried the refuted sentence next to the
  footnote contradicting it. Append-only records put the refuted text ABOVE its correction, so readers
  boot from it first. The records most likely to carry it are those that cited the artefact.
- An option was removed from a spec; two dependents (a pricing-table cell saying "cost dissolves", a
  sentence explaining a deferral) still asserted it in different words. Editing a source comment
  broke a verbatim quotation of it in a sibling document.

**How to apply:**

- Every rename/relocate/copy dispatch carries the question: "for each claim that travelled, is it
  true of the DESTINATION, or only of the source it was written for?" Verify copied claims at the
  primary, not on the source document's authority.
- When a landing strikes a claim, sweep for its other carriers immediately, by the claim's own
  vocabulary and by its CONSEQUENCES (a table cell, a cost column, a "therefore"), not by the
  artefact's name. Search for a third dependent; do not trust a dependent count.
- Strike each carrier in place with the old text visible, not by appending. Assert each replacement
  matched exactly once and refuse to write otherwise; a silent no-op leaves the claim standing while
  reporting success.
- A change to anything quoted elsewhere is not done until the quoters are re-checked mechanically.
