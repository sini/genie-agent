---

**Rule:** Record what the owner said, as said, and scope it to exactly what they ruled. A conditional
reply, a pivot or an unanswered offer is not a ruling. A ruling that something IS a defect does not
authorise any particular fix.

**Why:**

- An orchestrator banked a ruling in the same turn it answered the owner's "why?" that the ruling
  depended on. Owner: "You recorded a ruling without answering my question, a ruling that was
  contingent on an explanation." Both the record and a relay had to be retracted.
- An owner deferred judgement pending a census: "I don't want it marked deferred; record it as I have
  said it and schedule the census." A deferred status parks a row as "not now"; they meant "ruling
  waits on work that starts now".
- An owner pinned one config detail mid-design; the agent treated it as approval of the whole design
  ("for the record, that wasn't an approval"). Another time a turn ended with an offer, the reply said
  "let's implement the spec", and the agent made the offered amendment to a published document ("I
  didn't ask you to make that amendment").
- The owner ruled a double check was a defect. The orchestrator told the builder this covered adding a
  field to a sealed record. Owner: "I didn't explicitly authorize adding a field. I would want to
  understand the larger impact first."

**How to apply:**

- Bank owner words verbatim, with no arm ruled and status unchanged when they defer. Dispatch the work
  they named; put the question again with the new facts.
- Contingent reply ("(i), but why X?"): answer X in the final message, put the question again, bank
  only on the next reply.
- An offer the reply does not address is DEAD. Re-raise it or drop it; do not fold it into the next
  action. Touching a published or frozen artefact needs a directive naming that artefact.
- A hedge ("I don't know", "maybe", "it seems") or a critique of one arm is not a ruling, even when
  it implies a simpler rule. The orchestrator proposed a guard exemption; the owner answered "I don't
  know, … we may as well also screen" the other case. The orchestrator read the counter-point as the
  decision and sent a plan to an external agent. Owner: "Why did you send a plan … in response to me
  saying 'I don't know' without discussing the decision with me?" Lay out the arms and ask.
- Relaying a ruling to a builder: scope it to what was said. If the only sound fix changes a published
  surface, the builder stops and writes an impact section: what the pin protects, every reader, which
  law it touches, the saving. The owner then chooses.
- Delegated ruling ("decide as the expert, I'll accept it if it aligns"): bank the delegation and the
  stated criteria first, grill the scouts with numbered questions, file a ruling block with evidence,
  rejected arms with reasons, the counter-case and a line-by-line alignment check. Nothing encoding the
  arm lands before the owner can read it; tell them it reverts if misaligned.
- A small marker or flag name inside a construction the owner already ruled is yours to choose; prefer
  existing vocabulary and record it. Genuinely new named things (a library, repository, component) still
  go to the owner first.
- An owner-ruled name needs no provenance re-verification. Acquire only what the ruling asked for.
