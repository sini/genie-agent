---
name: relayed-is-not-measured
description: When passing on a linter diagnostic, a tracker status, a figure from a record, a peer's claim or a correction (yours or anyone's), or acting on a conditional finding ("if X, then Y") after X happens
type: practice
---

**Rule:** Tag every claim you did not just derive `[RELAYED <source>]`, not `[MEASURED]`. A
diagnostic, a status field and a figure in a dated block are snapshots of a moment that may be gone;
the evaluator, not the linter, is the authority on what code does. A correction is a new claim and
owes the same independent check as the claim it strikes, before striking it. A conditional finding's
consequent decays independently of its antecedent: when X happens, re-read the thing Y describes.

**Why:**

- An LSP `unused_binding` warning was true of a buffer BETWEEN two edits and false of the saved file;
  relaying it would have deleted a working control. Seeding the binding moved one dependent cell.
- An issue's newest text read like open work; it had been CLOSED for two days after a full
  spec-gate-build cycle. A spec dispatch was spent against it. Reading the tail is not checking status.
- An audit figure ("gap 7") was closed the same day by an 18-minute sweep, then carried live for ten
  days and relayed as measured into a brief.
- A reviewer ran 63 driven checks, all sound, then sent an unprompted correction inferred from a static
  read, wrong on both points and relayed onward as authoritative. One person made three corrections to
  one family of line references: the one driven was right, the two narrated were wrong, one inverting
  who had created a fact. A wrong correction arrives with the authority of a re-derivation.
- The inverse: a builder reported "one shared bound became twelve"; the orchestrator's grep said zero
  change. The claim was right; the grep matched scalar assignments and missed associative-array
  entries. A disagreement between a relayed claim and your reading is a fork, not a refutation.
- A scout wrote "if the ruling extends, site 8 stops being a plain re-point". It extended and the
  consequent was booked, but the construction had been removed five days earlier.
- A spec section read "BLOCKS on an owner reading in flight" five days after the ruling and three after
  its execution, both on the tracker row. That header went into a brief as a fence and steered the
  agent away from the clause most needing repair.
- A correction asserted a carrier row "would be filed"; a prior-art sweep showed the work already had
  its own spec. Filing would have planted a phantom obligation.

**How to apply:**

- Before dispatching against a tracked item, query its STATUS, not only its newest text. Where a spec
  and its row disagree on whether something is open, the row's NEWEST dated entry governs.
- Before relaying a tool's finding, drive it: seed what it claims is unused or wrong and see if anything
  moves. A tool's zero is not an absence until armed with a known positive in the same run.
- Re-derive any figure you rest an argument on, with the predicate beside it.
- The tell: a premise you can state confidently but cannot name a command for. It is `[RELAYED]` even
  when a command reproduces the artefact: that measures "the artefact says this", not "this is true".
- A provenance claim (who told whom, when) is checkable like a number: check it or do not state it.
- Detaching a consequent owes a measurement: not "did X happen" but "does what Y names still exist".
- A correction inherits the burden of what it corrects: replacing a population figure with a sampled
  one is a new defect dressed as a fix.
- A fence in a brief is as load-bearing as a premise. Retracting one in flight, say so plainly and tell
  the agent to weight your remaining premises accordingly.
- Run a prior-art sweep on the TOPIC before any new filing.
- When a result lacks something that could not possibly be absent, the instrument is broken.
- A search hit with an ellipsis is a pointer, not the field: open it and read the token whole before
  quoting a path, id, date or label.
